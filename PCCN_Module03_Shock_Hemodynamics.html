Deficit calculation

Fluid deficit (mL) = % dehydration × weight (kg) × 10

Example: 10 kg child, 5% dehydrated → 5 × 10 × 10 = 500 mL deficit, replaced alongside maintenance (Section 3).

FLUID IS A DRUG — KNOW THE DOSE

The 4 D's

DrugFluid IS the drug
DosemL/kg, matched to need
DurationHow fast, how long
De-escalationStop when endpoints met

Crystalloids

Isotonic (NS/RL) — resuscitation Hypotonic (0.45NS/D5W) — maintenance Hypertonic (3% saline) — raised ICP

Colloids

Albumin, blood products

Used in dengue shock & septic shock — large molecules pull fluid back into the vessels.

Resuscitation — FAST (push-pull, minutes)
→
Replacement — SLOW (hours)
→
Maintenance — SLOWER (restricted 50–70%)
Sick children over-secrete ADH → water retention & hyponatraemia. Restrict maintenance fluid to 50–70% of calculated need until enteral feeds are established.

ROSE — the phases of fluid therapy

Rescue — minutes, positive balance OK Optimization — hours, avoid overload Stabilization — days, zero/neutral balance Evacuation — days, negative balance, de-escalate

Watch for fluid overload

Worsening distressFalling SpO₂Basal crepitationsEnlarged liver

Monitor these endpoints

Skin turgorCRTUrine outputWeight trendMental statusLactate

Fluid overload %

(Total intake − Total output) ÷ Baseline weight × 100

>10% is concerning — reassess and consider de-escalation/diuretics.
Isotonic unless prescribed otherwise Crystalloids before colloids Monitor after every bolus Start de-escalation once endpoints met Early enteral feeds

Correct — 50–70%, due to the risk of ADH-driven water retention.

NIBP & Invasive Lines

Lecture: "NIBP — how to choose"

▶
Watch: NIBP — How to Choose~8 min · lifetime access

Cuff sizing

Bladder width ≈ 40% of arm circumference · length covers 80–100%

Measured midway between olecranon and acromion. Wrong size = wrong number — too small reads falsely high, too large reads falsely low.

When NIBP isn't enough — the arterial line

Continuous BP + easy sampling Sites: radial, femoral, dorsalis pedis Sterile technique ± ultrasound guidance Zero at the phlebostatic axis Square-wave test checks damping Flush regularly to prevent clotting
Needing >1 vasoactive drug is the trigger to insert an arterial line for continuous, accurate pressure monitoring — don't wait for things to get worse.

Indications

Continuous beat-to-beat BP monitoring Frequent ABG / blood sampling Vasoactive infusion titration Hemodynamic instability / anticipated shifts

Contraindications (relative)

Inadequate collateral flow (failed Allen's test — radial site) Local infection or thrombosis at the site Severe coagulopathy

Complications

Thrombosis Distal ischemia Infection Hemorrhage / hematoma Nerve injury Pseudoaneurysm

Source: OPENPediatrics "Arterial Line Placement" course — indications, contraindications, equipment, technique, sites, procedure, monitoring & documentation.

One arterial pressure cycle — rapid systolic upstroke, systolic peak, dicrotic notch (aortic valve closure), then diastolic runoff.

Reading the arterial waveform

Steep upstroke rate ≈ contractility (dP/dt) Dicrotic notch = aortic valve closure Diastolic runoff = coronary perfusion window Peripheral (radial) traces show a taller peak & later notch than central (aortic) ones — normal "pulse amplification," not hypertension

Invasive vs non-invasive BP monitoring

Invasive (arterial line)Non-invasive (NIBP cuff)
Continuous, beat-to-beatIntermittent, snapshot readings
Direct pressure measurementOscillometric — estimates BP from cuff pressure oscillations, not direct flow
Reliable in shock / low-flow statesProprietary algorithms vary by brand — readings differ device to device
Enables frequent blood samplingUnreliable with low stroke volume, arrhythmia, or patient movement
Never mix cuffs and tubing between different NIBP device brands — each is calibrated as a matched set, and swapping parts invalidates the reading.

Source: OPENPediatrics "Interpreting Arterial Pressure Waveforms" course — arterial system, dicrotic notch, invasive vs non-invasive monitoring.

Quick check — On an arterial waveform, the dicrotic notch corresponds to:
Correct — the dicrotic notch marks aortic valve closure, at the start of diastolic runoff.
Not quite — the dicrotic notch is caused by aortic valve closure.

Vasoactive drug classes

Inotropes↑ contractility — Adrenaline
Vasopressors↑ BP via constriction — Noradrenaline, Vasopressin
Inodilators↑ contractility + vasodilate — Dobutamine, Milrinone
RoutePeripheral to start, central for prolonged use
Adrenaline is the only vasoactive drug that can be given as a bolus — in arrest. All others: accurate infusion via pump, never bolused.
Quick check — What's the correct NIBP cuff bladder width, relative to arm circumference?
Correct — about 40% width, 80–100% length coverage.
Not quite — bladder width should be about 40% of arm circumference.

The Physiologically Difficult Airway

Lecture: "Physiologically difficult airway & peri-intubation resuscitation"

▶
Watch: The Physiologically Difficult Airway~15 min · lifetime access

RESUSCITATE BEFORE YOU INTUBATE

Why children desaturate faster

Low FRC High O₂ consumption Larger dead space Compliant chest wall, early muscle fatigue Can't breath-hold like an adult

Circulatory differences

High vagal tone → early bradycardia Low systemic vascular resistance Low total circulating volume Rapid drug metabolism — quicker onset, shorter duration

Spot the physiologically difficult airway

Any of these before intubation raises the risk of peri-intubation collapse:

High ICP RV dysfunction / failure Severe bronchospasm Severe pulmonary hypertension Post-ROSC Upper GI / airway bleed Acidosis Very low saturation Hypotension
~25% of children who are hemodynamically stable before intubation develop post-intubation hypotension (PIH) after RSI. Predict it before it happens — the Shock Index (HR ÷ SBP) is a quick bedside predictor.
PressureAccess, fluids, vasoactives — early
Pre-oxygenationSpO₂ >93%, PEEP, apneic O₂
pHDon't drop CO₂ fast, treat cause
PumpBedside echo — contractility & preload
The 4 P's of Peri-Intubation Resuscitation — work through all four before you push drugs.

Pressure — Resuscitation

AccessFluidsVasoactive agents early

MAP for age: 5th centile = 40 + 1.5×age(yrs) · 50th centile = 55 + 1.5×age(yrs)

Pre-oxygenation — Optimization

SpO₂ target >93%Correct position + good BMV techniquePEEP recruits alveoliApneic oxygenation — Rule of 15

If BMV pre-oxygenation fails, bridge through an LMA/supraglottic device before intubating — the Rapid Sequence Airway "middle path."

pH — Resuscitation

Preserve spontaneous breathing where possibleTreat underlying cause — bicarb is controversialFluid resuscitationMatch minute ventilation — don't drop CO₂ fast

Pump — rapid bedside assessment

4-chamber/parasternal long axis — contractilityIVC collapsibility — preloadOptional: VExUS, TAPSE

Work out whether the child is preload-dependent or afterload-dependent before choosing your drugs.

Drug of choice for the physiologically difficult airway: Ketamine + Rocuronium. If the child is too agitated to tolerate pre-oxygenation, consider Delayed Sequence Intubation instead of forcing RSI.

Case in practice — TBI with a Cushing reflex

6-month-old with TBI, initially stable, becomes unresponsive and stops protecting the airway; vitals now show a Cushing reflex.

CPP = MAP − ICP Laryngeal stimulation → BP spike → ↑ICP → worse CPP Premedicate: Ketamine+Propofol / Fentanyl+Propofol / Thiopentone Then Rocuronium + Lignocaine to blunt reflexes Back-up: head-elevated intubation position
Use CRASH/HOP killers to spot it early Resuscitate before you intubate Call for help early — not a weakness Aggressively pre-oxygenate Start pressor/inotrope via peripheral line early Most experienced intubator first, max 2 attempts Use a checklist and standard algorithms
Quick check — Besides Pressure, Pre-oxygenation and pH, the 4th "P" of peri-intubation resuscitation is:
Correct — Pressure, Pre-oxygenation, pH and Pump: a rapid bedside look at cardiac function before you push drugs.
Not quite — the 4th P is Pump: a quick bedside echo look at contractility and preload.

1-hour sepsis bundle walkthrough

Think sepsis: infection + temp ≥38°C/<36°C Rising O₂ need, altered mental state Prolonged CRT, mottled/ashen skin

Within the first hour

Cultures before antibiotics Broad-spectrum antibiotic (e.g. Ceftriaxone 100mg/kg) Fluid bolus — push-pull within 5 min if hypotensive Vasoactive if fluid-refractory Consider stress-dose hydrocortisone (1–2mg/kg) if at risk

Fluid Resuscitation: Deficit, Maintenance, Replacement

Calculation workshop

Maintenance (4-2-1 rule): 4 mL/kg/hr for the first 10 kg + 2 mL/kg/hr for the next 10 kg + 1 mL/kg/hr per kg after.
Worked example — 20 kg child: (4×10) + (2×10) = 60 mL/hr full maintenance → restricted to 50–70% while sick = 30–42 mL/hr.
Peripheral vasoactive drip without a pump — Adrenaline/Noradrenaline 0.15 mg/kg in 250 mL saline, run at 10 mL/hr ≈ 0.1 mcg/kg/min (for a child >15 kg).
