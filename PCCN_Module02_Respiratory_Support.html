Distress vs Failure

Lecture: "Respiratory Distress vs Failure"

▶
Watch: Respiratory Distress vs Failure~10 min · lifetime access

CATCH IT IN DISTRESS — NOT FAILURE

DISTRESS
still compensating
→
IMPENDING FAILURE
losing the fight
→
ARREST

Distress looks like

Airway maintained Effort visible Stridor / wheeze Tachycardic Pale, perfused Irritable / agitated

Failure looks like

Sounds fading to silent Bradypnoea → apnoea Minimal chest rise HR falling Cyanosis Lethargic → unresponsive

Upper airway

Croup, FB, epiglottitis

Stridor

Lower airway

Asthma, bronchiolitis

Wheeze

Lung tissue

Pneumonia, ARDS

Crackles, grunt

Control of breathing

CNS, TBI, sedation

Irregular pattern
Mnemonic: Inspiratory stridor → upper. Expiratory wheeze → lower.
Quick check — Wheeze, decreased air movement, no stridor. Which category?
Correct — wheeze, no stridor, reduced entry = lower airway.
Not quite — wheeze + no stridor = lower airway obstruction.

Bag-Mask Ventilation

Lecture: "Bag Mask Ventilation"

▶
Watch: Bag Mask Ventilation~14 min · lifetime access

THE SKILL IS THE SEAL

🟢 Self-inflating bag

First choiceNot if breathing spontaneously

🔵 Flow-inflating bag

More training neededManages flow + seal + volume together
Infant/small child bag: 450 mL min Older child bag: up to 1000 mL 2-person technique if seal is hard
Under-ventilation → respiratory acidosis. Volume over speed.
Pre-oxygenate (HFNC/BVM/Bain) to SpO₂ >93% for ≥3 min before escalating.
Quick check — Which bag-mask should NOT be used on a spontaneously breathing child?
Correct — self-inflating is first choice for resuscitation, but not for a spontaneously breathing child.
Not quite — it's the self-inflating bag to avoid.

High-Flow Nasal Cannula

Lecture: "HFNC"

▶
Watch: HFNC~14 min · lifetime access

HFNC BUYS TIME — IT'S NOT A CURE

✅ Use it for

SpO₂ <90% Mod–severe WOB CCF / fluid overload Post-op (T&A) Chronic lung disease Weaning from CPAP

🚫 Don't use it for

Craniofacial anomaly Recent nasal/facial surgery Air leak syndrome Life-threatening hypoxia Haemodynamic instability GCS <8

Flow formula

1–2 L/kg/min (first 12 kg) + 0.5–1 L/kg/min (each kg after)

20 kg example: 24 + 4 = 28 L/min (up to 32). Target SpO₂ 94–99%. Never occlude nares >50–60%.

Watch continuously

ConsciousnessRR / WOBHR / perfusionSpO₂

Check intermittently

Nares/cheeks skinAbdominal distensionFluid balance
HFNC is an aerosol-generating procedure — PPE on, humidifier cleaned between patients.

Is it working? (2 hr check)

HR ↓20%RR ↓20%Distress improvingFiO₂ ≤40%

Wean FiO₂ first (to 92–97%) then flow, or flow first — then step down to room air/LFNC.

Failing: FiO₂ >80% to hold saturation, WOB not improving, worsening acidosis → escalate now, don't wait.
Case in practice

Reactive airway disease, worsening despite oxygen

Cannula 2L
→
NRM 10L
→
HFNC
→
Failing → NIV/intubate

Each device buys time, not a cure — escalate on trend, not on one number.

Quick check — Child on HFNC, good SpO₂, now drowsy with minimal chest rise. Do what?
Correct — drowsiness + poor chest rise precede the SpO₂ drop. Escalate now.
Not quite — don't wait for SpO₂ to fall.
