Arrest Rhythms — Shockable or Not?

Lecture: "Reading the Arrest Rhythm"

▶
Watch: Reading the Arrest Rhythm~11 min · lifetime access

NO PULSE? THE MONITOR TELLS YOU SHOCK OR DON'T

No pulse found — check the rhythm on the monitor
↓
Shockable — VT / VF
⚡ Defibrillate immediately
CPR 2 min, resume
Epinephrine q3–5min
Amiodarone if refractory
Not Shockable — Asystole / PEA
🚫 Do NOT shock
CPR 2 min, resume
Epinephrine q3–5min
Search & treat the cause
Call for help / activate Code Blue and start CPR the moment pulse is confirmed absent — don't wait for the rhythm diagnosis to begin compressions.

Nursing actions during any arrhythmia

1. Assess ABCs 2. Artefact or true rhythm? 3. Call for help / Code Blue 4. Start CPR if no pulse 5. Prepare defib/cardioversion 6. Drugs per PALS
Quick check — Pulseless VT is found on the monitor. What's the immediate next step?
Correct — pulseless VT is shockable. Defibrillate now, don't wait.
Not quite — pulseless VT is shockable and needs immediate defibrillation.

Bedside checklist for any new rhythm: 1) Sinus? Every QRS after a normal P. 2) Rate, QRS width, PR, regularity. 3) Pulses/SpO₂/hemodynamics first, whatever the rhythm. 4) Correct acidosis, sugar, temp, electrolytes (Ca/K/Mg). 5) Know your drugs. 6) Defibrillator ready.
Quick check — Narrow-complex, regular, fast rhythm with a pulse, in a distressed infant. Most likely?
Correct — narrow, regular, fast, with a pulse = SVT until proven otherwise.
Not quite — narrow + regular + fast + pulse present points to SVT.

Crash cart & code preparedness

Cart at the foot of the bed; one nurse manages it (drugs, flushes, syringes); document everything. Per-shift check: drawers sealed, drug expiry current, defib/BVM/O₂ functional. After a code: restock immediately, log use.

Post-Cardiac-Surgery Arrhythmias

Lecture: "The First 72 Hours After Repair"

▶
Watch: The First 72 Hours After Repair~10 min · lifetime access

Junctional Ectopic Tachycardia (JET)

Narrow-complex, AV dissociation First 24–72 hr post-repair Poorly tolerated

Cool the patient, optimise sedation/analgesia, involve cardiology/intensivist early — avoid agents that increase automaticity.

Complete Heart Block

Near the conduction system e.g. after VSD closure

Keep temporary pacing wires and a pacing plan readily available.

New post-op tachyarrhythmia: check narrow vs wide QRS → temp/pulse/BP/glucose/electrolytes → 12-lead ECG + repeat echo → inform cardiologist → prep rate/rhythm-control drugs with the defibrillator ready.
Quick check — A narrow-complex tachycardia with AV dissociation appears 36 hours after VSD repair. Most likely?
Correct — JET is the classic early post-repair rhythm with AV dissociation.
Not quite — this pattern in the first 24–72 hr post-repair is JET.
